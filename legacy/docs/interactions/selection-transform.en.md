# Selection and Transform Contract

[中文](selection-transform.md) | English

## Creating Selections

- Rectangle/ellipse selections and matching shapes can use a fixed drawing anchor. The default is the canvas center; nine-position presets, X/Y controls, and canvas dragging adjust it, with a separate visibility toggle. The anchor is stored relative to canvas size and resolved on the half-pixel grid. With fixed-center drawing enabled, preview and committed geometry use the same anchor; these tool preferences do not enter document undo history.

- Rectangle, ellipse, free lasso, polygon lasso, and magic wand share Replace, Add, Subtract, and Intersect modes.
- All five selection tools share horizontal, vertical, upper-right-to-lower-left, and upper-left-to-lower-right symmetry axes. Every new original mask first computes and deduplicates its closure through all enabled axes and the movable pivot, then combines with the previous selection according to Replace, Add, Subtract, or Intersect. Path preview, magic-wand preview, and committed result must match. Diagonal mirrored pixels outside a rectangular canvas do not enter the mask.
- With symmetry enabled, moving, scaling, rotating, or skewing transforms only one representative region from each symmetry orbit and regenerates the mirrored closure around the current pivot. A move may begin from any mirrored region; the pressed region follows the pointer while all other regions move with the corresponding reflection in real time. Outline, pixel preview, actual write, and the single undo entry must agree. Already symmetric source content must not be copied again into extra duplicates.
- Polygon lasso adds a vertex on click. Clicking the first vertex, double-clicking the last point, or pressing `Enter` closes and commits; `Esc` cancels. Before closure, middle mouse or `Space` temporarily pans the canvas. Existing edges and the pointer's final segment remain visible during and after pan, and existing vertices, combination mode, and original selection remain available for continued boundary creation. When Preferences > Editing > Optimized Line Algorithm is enabled, preview and final edges between vertices use even pixel steps of similar lengths. Preferences > Editing controls whether free-lasso drag preview closes by default.
- Right-button drag subtracts by default. Beginning a selection while holding `Shift` bypasses movement, scale, and rotation hit testing of the existing selection so Add can begin inside it. The tool-options bar temporarily shows Add while `Shift` is held and Subtract while the right button is held, then restores the user's selected combination mode. After a transform begins, `Shift` remains an aspect or angle constraint.
- While creating rectangle, ellipse, or lasso selection, show only the new path's pixel preview inside the canvas. Do not show selection borders or transform points for the new result. Existing selection borders remain visible during Replace, Add, Subtract, and Intersect and the combined result appears only once on release. Rectangle and ellipse path preview automatically chooses black or white against the underlying color by default and may be changed to a fixed custom color in Preferences.
- During rectangle or ellipse drag, the bottom application status bar shows current `width x height` in real time and returns to committed selection information on release or cancel. The existing canvas-size indicator inside the canvas remains unchanged. A fixed-screen-size two-line information block also appears above the selection's top-left start point: line one shows inclusive start and end coordinates side by side, and line two shows `width x height`. It remains horizontally readable through rotation, mirroring, and view transforms, with a translucent gray background and white text. If top or right space is insufficient, move it inside the viewport while keeping the top-left anchor. Hide it on release. Preferences > Show Marquee Size controls only this floating block and does not disable real-time bottom status information.
- In Replace mode, a press and release outside an existing selection with no real drag clears the selection; pointer down alone must not clear it early. Click classification uses CSS pointer movement distance. If a real drag occurs, commit a one-pixel selection even when down and up remain in the same document pixel.
- Double-clicking the canvas with the rectangular marquee quickly selects the unit under the pointer. On the second press, immediately show a solid preview of the whole unit without waiting for release and without selection borders. Holding the second press and dragging expands the preview through every complete unit crossed and snaps to unit boundaries rather than creating a free pixel rectangle. Replace, Add, Subtract, and Intersect always combine against the selection before the operation. Show final selection borders only after release. With a custom grid enabled, use its origin and unit width and height; otherwise use transparency checkerboard size. Incomplete units at canvas edges clip to valid canvas bounds.
- When Add mode is explicitly selected in the tool options, content inside an existing selection can still move and scale handles, rotation zones, and edge movement remain active. Subtract and Intersect begun inside the selection prioritize combination selection. Temporary `Shift` Add and right-button Subtract always bypass transform hit testing. Lasso combination continues showing pixel preview.
- The pointer may begin outside the canvas and continue dragging on the document pixel grid, but creation preview draws only inside valid canvas bounds and the final mask contains no outside-canvas pixels.
- With tiled preview enabled, rectangle and ellipse drags use continuous document coordinates in the current duplicate and do not jump back or leave the pointer at a duplicate boundary. The same live preview appears in every visible duplicate. When the outline extends beyond the original canvas, draw one continuous complete outer boundary without showing tiled seams as internal edges. Only on release is the periodic result folded back into the original canvas selection. Duplicates store no extra selection state.
- While the magic wand is held, preview every pixel that will be selected. Dark pixels use a light preview and light pixels use a dark preview.
- A rotated ellipse selection is rasterized directly in the rotated local coordinate system. It must not generate a horizontal ellipse mask and nearest-neighbor rotate it. Diagonal ellipse boundaries remain continuous, symmetric, and consistent in thickness.
- A rotated rectangle selection uses strict pixel-center boundary rules. Thick diagonal rectangles remove isolated corner tips connected by only one orthogonal neighbor. Horizontal, vertical, and thin-line rectangles do not apply this cleanup.
- Pixels traversed by a lasso path belong to the final selection boundary. Closing must not consistently lose one row or column on the right or bottom because of half-open polygon tests.
- `Ctrl+A` selects the whole canvas.
- `Alt`-left-clicking an animation cell in the timeline switches to that layer and frame and selects every non-transparent pixel in the cell. `Shift+Alt`-click adds those pixels to the existing selection. Replacing from an empty cell clears selection; adding an empty cell keeps it unchanged. The selection change is undoable.
- Selection-tool hover draws compact square-corner marks at the four corners of the current document pixel, including outside canvas content bounds, without switching to a prohibited pointer. Marks use fixed CSS-pixel line width independent of view zoom and cover at least `8 x 8 CSS px`, expanding around the center when the pixel is smaller. The mouse itself is hidden by default and only the four marks appear. When Preferences > Cursor > Show Crosshair While Selecting is enabled, show a true mouse crosshair and do not draw another cross inside the marks. `Shift` Add retains the marks. Scale, rotate, and edge-move hit regions hide them.
- `Ctrl+Shift+I` inverts the current selection inside canvas bounds and creates one undoable history step. With no selection, show a message and create no history.
- `Ctrl+H` only hides or shows selection borders. Eight transform controls remain visible and interactive. The real selection continues constraining drawing, adjustments, and movement. This view state does not enter document history.

## Hit Testing and Movement

- Without a canvas selection or floating transform, `Shift+H` / `Shift+V` mirror explicitly selected timeline content horizontally or vertically in one batch. Selected cells affect only those cells; selecting only layers or groups affects all their frames. An ordinary frame-header click replaces the previous layer selection, including automatic selection after pasting, and affects all editable layers in the selected frames. Adding frames with Ctrl/Shift after selecting layers affects their intersection. An implicit active layer affects only its current frame. Raster pixels mirror within each cel surface without moving it; Tilemap mirrors cells and tile orientation, while free tiles mirror instances without changing shared sources. Shared linked content is processed once; hidden, locked, and text layers are skipped. Selection and the active frame are preserved, and the whole batch uses one undo/redo step. Existing canvas selections, floating transforms, and explicitly selected free-tile instances retain their existing priority.

- Inside the selection moves selected content by default. Only an expanded hit region around the real selection border moves the outline itself. Empty edges of a non-rectangular selection's bounding rectangle must not trigger outline movement.
- Inner and outer hit widths around a selection edge are visually symmetric and all four sides trigger reliably. Hit distance uses continuous document coordinates and must not shift right or down because of pixel flooring.
- While creating, moving, or transforming a selection, reaching or crossing a canvas viewport edge continuously pans the view so the operation can continue. This pan is view state and does not enter document history.
- Existing selection movement, scale, rotation, and skew may place outline and mask partly or entirely outside the canvas. Only a newly created selection clips to canvas bounds. An out-of-bounds transform must not crop the mask early.
- With tiled preview enabled, an existing selection and floating content may be hit from any visible duplicate. Movement uses continuous document coordinates of the duplicate under the pointer. Crossing a duplicate seam or the outermost duplicate must not reverse, stop, or crop floating content. Even after internal target coordinates cross one or more canvas periods, live preview matches the final result. Confirming a pure move folds out-of-bounds pixels and selection back into the original canvas on enabled tiled axes, and still saves only one selection and one history entry.
- Transform hit priority is fixed: eight white scale handles, compact four-corner rotation zones, selection-edge move band, selection interior, then ordinary lasso area. Four edge midpoints use a 12 CSS px hit radius. Each corner retains an 18 CSS px easy target inside the selection and only 5 CSS px in the outer quadrant, covering the visible `8x8` handle and immediate tolerance. Crossing the handle's outer edge enters rotation immediately. The rotation zone extends at most 28 CSS px from the corner; beyond it, restore ordinary lasso input. Every radius converts strictly from CSS pixels at current zoom and must not be forced to at least one document pixel.
- Only selection tools show scale controls. Switching to Pencil or another drawing tool keeps only selection borders and still constrains drawing to the selection.
- Hide pixel preview when entering a scale, edge-move, or rotation hit region so operation hints do not overlap.
- Content remains movable when the selection covers the whole canvas.
- The first content move enters floating state and captures stable content and background sources. Until confirmation or cancellation, every subsequent move rebuilds from that first source and restores original background along traversed areas. It must not recapture the moved destination as a new source.
- After selecting multiple ordinary pixel layers or groups, marquee and `Ctrl+T` use one shared selection. The initial `Ctrl+T` bounds are the union of content bounds across all target layers; groups expand to every descendant layer, and original layer multi-selection remains. Move, scale, rotate, skew, copy, and mirror capture and write each layer separately without merging layers or changing hierarchy or order. Confirmation creates one undo for all layers and selection; cancel restores all layers together. If any target is text or Tilemap, block the entire transform instead of modifying only ordinary pixel layers.
- When multiple timeline frames are selected, selection movement and later floating transforms apply to the Cartesian combination of all selected ordinary pixel layers and selected frames. Groups expand to descendants. Frame and layer selections remain and do not convert into cel selection. Each target cel captures and writes independently; linked cels sharing the same real source are processed once per owning layer. If any target layer is text, Tilemap, free-tile, hidden, or locked, block the entire operation. Beginning from the canvas with marquee or Move preserves current layer and frame multi-selection. Onion skins for affected previous and next frames refresh during move preview. Confirm, cancel, undo, and redo cover every target and the whole operation creates one history step.
- A free-tile layer permits selection tools only when one visible, unlocked instance is selected and its source is unlocked. A new selection first clips to that instance's current rotated or mirrored bounds. During move, scale, rotate, skew, or copy, selection stays in document coordinates for the current instance; pixel transforms execute in a temporary source raster generated for the instance's display orientation and inverse-map into the shared source. Content-move preview refreshes in the same pointer update without waiting another frame. Every same-source instance refreshes only its pixels according to its own instance transform and does not duplicate selection, borders, pivot, or controls. Confirmation undoes source pixels and selection geometry together; cancellation restores both.
- Horizontal or vertical mirroring of a floating selection flips pixel values, non-rectangular mask, selected offset, and move-preview cache together. The original clear range remains separate and unchanged, so moving after repeated mirrors must not restore pre-mirror content.
- When Move shifts the active layer or multi-selected layers, an existing selection follows the same displacement. One undo or redo restores both layer offsets and selection position.
- `Shift` constrains horizontally or vertically and dynamically chooses the main axis from relative displacement.
- `Ctrl`-dragging selected content copies it. An ordinary move or already copied floating content may use `Ctrl` again: first confirm the current position, then begin a new copied move from there. Each confirmed copied move is undone by one Undo.

## Stroke Selection

- `Shift+O` opens Stroke Selection. While open, `Enter` always applies the stroke and closes the dialog even when keyboard focus remains on the canvas. The same key must not also confirm the selection.
- Stroke color, width, position, pixel directions, quick shape, and live-preview toggle are current-project settings. After a successful apply, they are stored in `.moonsprite` and restored on the next open.
- Before ordinary tool input or marquee creation, `Alt` retains temporary color picking and is not used to copy a selection. After rectangle or ellipse drag has begun, `Alt` changes to adjustment of the current selection's rotation direction.

## Scale and Rotation

- Four corner scale handles remain visible above content.
- Every tool shows a fixed `18 x 18 CSS px` black-and-white pivot marker for a valid ordinary selection. It uses a 9x9 SVG pixel icon at integer `2x` scale and defaults to the single document-pixel center of the transformed selection; even width or height uses the right or lower center pixel. The marker has screen size independent of zoom and stays precisely aligned after view rotation or mirroring. Hover retains the software default pointer; pressing and moving changes to Move. Dragging steps by one document pixel and changes only session interaction state, not pixels or document history. The selection tool options provide nine presets: upper-left, top, upper-right, left, center, right, lower-left, bottom, and lower-right. Each lands on the center of one corresponding pixel inside the selection, not a pixel-boundary intersection. The marker can be hidden independently. Hiding affects display and hit testing only; the pivot still participates in rotation, scale, and skew and the toggle does not enter document history.
- A manually placed pivot applies to later rotation and skew. During transform, pivot is fixed while outline, eight controls, mask, and pixels transform around it. Moving the whole selection or moving it with a layer moves the custom pivot by the same displacement. Ordinary scale preserves the pivot's relative position inside the selection and moves it with live preview; crossing an edge mirrors it instead of resetting to center. During `Alt` pivot scaling, pivot remains at its original document coordinate. Creating, replacing, clearing the selection, or switching animation frame resets it to the new selection center.
- Dragging an existing scale handle with `Alt` fixes the current pivot and transforms opposite sides symmetrically around it. An axis flips only after crossing the pivot. Without manual movement, the pivot is the default selection center. `Shift` preserves the original aspect ratio. `Ctrl` snaps width and height independently to integer multiples of original pixel dimensions. All three modifiers can combine and preview updates immediately if they are pressed or released while the pointer remains down. A scale-handle operation must not copy content because of `Ctrl` or enter temporary color pick because of `Alt`.
- With a rectangle or ellipse selection tool, after pointer-down begins a drag, `Ctrl` selects symmetrically around the initial press, `Shift` preserves 1:1, and `Ctrl+Shift` creates a centered 1:1 selection. Holding `Alt` adjusts only the current rectangle's rotation direction. If the left button remains held and `Alt` is released after changing the angle, the pointer immediately resumes width and height control around the rotation center at the release moment, applying one-sided radius pixel changes symmetrically to the opposite side without center drift caused by diagonal projection rounding. Pressing `Ctrl` after an `Alt` angle change preserves the exact current angle and dimensions, temporarily returns the center to the initial press, and continues symmetric adjustment from the current pointer. `Ctrl` alone must not add a 1:1 constraint; only `Shift` or an explicit fixed-ratio shape setting does. Releasing `Ctrl` restores the boundary, direction, and center from before `Ctrl` was pressed and uses the current pointer at release as the baseline for subsequent adjustment. The temporary centered result must not persist. `Space` moves the current shape from the pointer position at press without changing to the hand cursor and must not jump when adjustment resumes after release. Final selection on mouse release exactly matches the last preview.
- Rectangle and ellipse shape tools reuse the same `Ctrl`, `Shift`, `Alt`, and `Space` interactions. Rotated fill and outline use the same pixel raster result for preview and commit. Rectangles must not regain isolated corner tips, and ellipse outlines must not fill the center.
- When a selection exists and no text input or dialog control has focus, an arrow key moves selection content and boundary by one document pixel. The first key enters the same floating state as mouse drag. Repeated keys rebuild from the first captured content and background. Confirmation turns the continuous sequence into one document-history entry; cancellation restores original content and all traversed background.
- Rotation hit zones are outside the four corners and separate from scale handles.
- Rotation zones show no extra blue control. Each sits directly outside a diagonal scale zone and stays within 28 CSS px of the corner. Leaving the outer 5 CSS px diagonal-scale region enters rotation immediately with no default-pointer gap. Beyond the compact rotation zone, restore ordinary lasso input so selection can still begin near an existing selection.
- Holding `Shift` while rotating snaps to eight directions every 45°, including 0°, 45°, and 90°. Release restores continuous rotation.
- Even when the selection contains no non-transparent layer pixels, its boundary may move, scale, rotate, and skew. Such an operation creates no pixel edit, but confirmation records one undoable selection change.
- During rotation drag, hide selection borders temporarily. Eight scale controls rotate with the transformed rectangle and angle. On release, restore borders, but controls remain at final rotated positions and hit along rotated local axes until floating transform is confirmed or canceled. Corner rotation cursors are chosen dynamically from each control's displayed direction relative to transform center rather than fixed original upper-left or upper-right semantics. A control near the vertical center axis uses the upper or lower rotation pointer accordingly.
- After confirming rotation, scale, or skew, crop every fully unselected row and column around the final mask. Eight controls, hit regions, and the next transform use this tight range and must not keep surrounding blank area from the transform rectangle.
- A compact skew hit zone exists outside each edge-midpoint scale zone, extends at most 28 CSS px like rotation, and shows no extra controls. Skew direction follows the corresponding edge of the current quadrilateral: top and bottom follow the current top-edge direction; left and right follow the current side-edge direction. The pointer selects among horizontal, vertical, upper-left-to-lower-right, and upper-right-to-lower-left skew icons according to displayed direction. Consecutive drags on different edges accumulate from the current quadrilateral and pivot, never return to original rectangle coordinates, and never reset previous skew. During drag, the pointer changes to Move. Selection mask, pixel preview, final content, and the single undo entry share one skew mapping.
- Transform outline, content, and preview use the same transform matrix.
- A scale handle may cross the opposite edge. Horizontal crossing toggles horizontal mirroring, vertical crossing toggles vertical mirroring, and crossing both toggles both. The flip axis comes from the dragged edge. For a diagonal corner crossing, horizontal and vertical axes use the two coordinates of the dragged corner rather than the target rectangle center. Preview, commit, and one undo use the same flip state.
- View rotation changes display only and does not change document selection or transform coordinates.
- While an adjustment dialog is open, selection movement, transform, Add, and Subtract first remove temporary adjusted-pixel preview and then reapply the same adjustment values to the new selection or transform result. Adjustment values remain unchanged, and background or unselected pixels are not adjusted.

## Paste and Outside-Canvas Content

- Internal copies retain the original selection coordinates. An axis keeps its original coordinate while the copied selection center remains visible on that axis; an off-screen axis is centered independently in the current viewport. External images are centered in the currently visible canvas area, and repeated pastes do not add a cascade offset. Content smaller than the canvas may extend outside it but keeps at least one pixel inside; content at least as large as the canvas on either axis is constrained from the canvas origin side.
- Paste enters movable state by default and retains complete content without clipping to current canvas bounds early.
- Layer data may have negative offsets or extend beyond the canvas. Pixels remain when moved back.
- Only an explicit Trim Outside Canvas operation may permanently delete outside-bound pixels.
